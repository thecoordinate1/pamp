# PAMP supply-side plan and positioning groundwork

Date: 2026-10-04. **Nothing here has been posted or sent.** All of it is draft
for approval. Assumptions are tagged `[A1]`…`[A14]` and listed at the end.

Why supply-side and not a launch campaign: production has one published event,
already past, with 6 RSVPs. Driving consumer traffic now lands people on an
empty Explore tab. Payments do not work, so nothing may promise paid ticketing.

---

## 1. Positioning

**Core benefit.** Know exactly who is coming to your event: guests RSVP, you
approve them, and you scan them in at the door with a QR pass.

*Attendee-side:* Find what's on in your city, get on the guest list, and walk in
with a pass that works without data.

**Positioning statement.** PAMP helps Lusaka event hosts run a guest list they
can trust by giving every event one shareable link with RSVP, host approval,
hidden exact location, QR passes and door check-in. It is free.

**Campaign angle: "Your place. Your list."** The tension is the gap between "80
people said they're coming in the WhatsApp group" and the 25 who show up — and
the reverse, a host who doesn't want an open invite to their address. Every
mechanic that answers this already works: RSVP gives a real count,
request-to-join gives control, the approximate pin gives privacy, single-use
passes and door scanning give a trustworthy headcount. No payments required.

### Discovery vs networking — recommendation

**Lead with events and the guest list. Demote "networking hub" to an outcome.**

1. Zambians find events via WhatsApp statuses, Facebook posts, flyers and word of
   mouth, and search "what's on this weekend". Nobody searches for a "networking
   hub". `[A3]`
2. "Networking" reads corporate — business breakfasts and LinkedIn. It will put
   off the house-party, student and church-youth hosts you need first, and it
   contradicts a brand called *Party at My Place*.
3. "Hub" is a claim about supply. With one past event it is not credible to a
   host who checks, and it invites a comparison you currently lose.
4. Networking needs no extra feature — it happens when people attend events.
   "Find the party. Meet your people." already puts discovery first and people
   second. That sequence is right.
5. Hosts are the scarce side, and "networking" is a weak pitch to someone
   planning an event. Control and certainty is the pitch.

**Concrete changes.** Retire "Zambia's events & networking hub" as the live
positioning line. Replace with **"Events in Zambia, with a proper guest list."**
(alternate: "Free events, real guest lists.") `[A4]` Reintroduce "networking"
later only as one category among many.

## 2. Host acquisition plan — target 5–10 real upcoming Lusaka events in 2–3 weeks

**Principle: concierge listing.** You create the listing from the host's flyer in
~10 minutes, then hand over the link. This removes the biggest objection, which
is effort. Hosts get value before learning the app. *Open question `[A5]`: can
the app create an event on a host's behalf, or transfer ownership?*

**Honesty rule on payments.** Never offer or imply ticket selling. If asked:
"PAMP doesn't take payments. You collect at the door like you do now. PAMP
handles the list and the check-in." List free-entry events first. Whether to
list pay-at-the-door events is a user decision `[A6]`.

### First-host archetypes and where to find them

Venue/campus names are common Lusaka reference points — verify before use `[A7]`.

1. **House-party and private-venue hosts** — the literal brand fit. Birthday,
   rooftop, braai and yard events; they care who walks in. Find via Instagram and
   Facebook posts tagged with Lusaka suburbs (Kabulonga, Roma, Woodlands,
   Northmead, Kabwata) and your own network. Strongest "approval + hidden
   location" use case.
2. **University and college societies** — UNZA, Cavendish, ZCAS, Mulungushi
   Lusaka campuses. Free or cheap socials. Tech-friendly, data-conscious,
   group-driven. Find via society Facebook pages, class WhatsApp groups, campus
   noticeboards.
3. **Church youth and ministry events** — free entry, well attended. QR passes are
   a headcount tool. Position as a planning tool, not a party app.
4. **Small promoters and DJ collectives** already posting flyers weekly. Search
   Facebook/Instagram for "Lusaka events this weekend", "Lusaka DJ", and see who
   tags venues. They worry about crowd control and no-shows.
5. **Comedy, open-mic, poetry and music nights** — small rooms need exact numbers.
6. **Run clubs, fitness groups, markets, food pop-ups** — free and recurring, so
   one host seeds several listings.
7. **Professional and tech meetups** — want a guest list and check-in; also tests
   the networking angle. Do these last.

### Sourcing method (free, ~2 hours)

Search Facebook Events and pages for "Lusaka" across the next 30 days; Instagram
hashtags and location tags; your own WhatsApp statuses and groups. Build a Google
Sheet of 40 names: name, archetype, event date, contact route, what they use now.
Rank by event date — only hosts with an event in the next 2–3 weeks can become a
listing in time.

**Funnel math `[A8]`:** 40 contacted → ~15 reply → ~8 agree → 5–8 listings live.
If response runs below this, widen the list rather than lowering the bar.

### Three-week schedule

- **Days 1–2** — build the 40-name sheet. Finalise DM and one-pager; get approval.
  Create 1–2 anchor listings from your own network so there is something to show.
- **Days 3–7** — 20 DMs by hand, 5–8/day. Ask for a reply, not a signup. Reply
  within the hour. Concierge-list every yes the same day.
- **Days 8–14** — next 20 DMs. One follow-up after 3 days with a different angle
  (offline QR pass, or the hidden address). First public post naming a real host,
  with permission.
- **Days 15–21** — second wave on the archetype that converted best. Ask every
  live host for one introduction. Check the trigger metrics.
- **Always** — attend each event or get host feedback within 24 hours. A host with
  a good door experience is the best source of the next three hosts.

### What PAMP offers that a WhatsApp group does not

All verified working:

- A real headcount — RSVP is a commitment step; "count me in" is not.
- A choice over who comes — request-to-join with host approval.
- Privacy for your address — approximate pin, exact location revealed on request.
- A pass per guest — QR, saved offline, valid once.
- Plus-ones without chaos — single-use shareable pass links.
- A door that moves — real QR check-in, with undo.
- Reach beyond your group — discoverable by city, category and search.
- One link that previews properly in WhatsApp and Facebook (v0.13.0).
- Referral codes to track who brought people.
- Free, no app download (installable PWA).

*The WhatsApp group stays the right place to chat. PAMP is where the list lives.*

### Likely objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "My people are all on WhatsApp." | Keep the group. Put the PAMP link in it. RSVPs and approvals in PAMP, chat stays in the group. |
| "Nobody uses PAMP yet." | True — say so. It works as your own guest-list link regardless. Early hosts get set up personally. |
| "Will it cost me?" | No. Hosting and RSVP are free. Make no promise about future pricing. |
| "Can I sell tickets?" | No. PAMP doesn't take payments. Collect at the door as you do today. |
| "My guests won't download an app." | No download — it opens as a web page, installable to the home screen. |
| "Data is expensive, network is bad." | The QR pass is saved on the phone and works offline. |
| "I don't want strangers at my house." | That's exactly why approval and the hidden address exist. |
| "Sounds like work." | We create the listing from your flyer. Send flyer, date, place. |
| "Is this legit? Who are you?" | Name the founder, show the live site and the first event. Offer a call or voice note. |
| "I already use Facebook Events." | Facebook "Going" is not a commitment and has no door check-in. Use PAMP for the door, keep posting on Facebook. `[A9]` |
| "What if the scan fails?" | There's undo, and the guest list as fallback. Test the scan with the host beforehand. |

## 3. Host outreach copy

### A. WhatsApp DM (manual sending only)

Casual Zambian English, short, no marketing language. Personal message, never a
broadcast. Local-language greetings are a user choice `[A10]`.

**Cold** (saw their event online):
> Hi [name], saw your [event] on [Facebook/Instagram], looks good. I'm [your
> name], I'm building PAMP, a free Lusaka events app where guests RSVP, you
> approve them and scan them in with a QR at the door. Would you like me to put
> [event] on there for you? I'll do it from your flyer, takes me 10 mins, free.
> No pressure, even just to see how it looks.

**Warm** (someone you know):
> Hey [name]! You hosting anything soon? I made something that might help with
> the "how many people are actually coming" problem. Guests RSVP on one link, you
> pick who gets in, and you scan them at the door. Free. Send me your flyer and
> I'll set it up, you can tell me if it's useful.

**Follow-up after 3 days** (one only):
> Hi [name], just checking you saw this. If you're still planning [event], send
> the flyer, date and place and I'll have the link ready today. If it's not for
> you, no problem.

**If they say yes:**
> Nice. Send me: the flyer, date and time, area, and if it's free entry. I'll
> send you the link to check before it goes live.

**If they ask about tickets:**
> PAMP doesn't take payments, so you'd collect at the door like normal. It
> handles the guest list and check-in.

Rules: never send identical text to more than one person in a group. Don't
message after a "no". Never claim user numbers.

### B. One-page pitch (60-second read)

> **List your event on PAMP. Free.**
>
> **Know who's actually coming.**
>
> PAMP is a free events app for Zambia. Guests RSVP to your event, you approve
> who gets in, and you scan them at the door.
>
> **The problem you already know**
> - Everyone says "I'm coming" in the group chat, and half don't show.
> - You can't post your exact address for everyone to see.
> - At the door it's a paper list, a phone and an argument.
>
> **What you get**
> - One link for your event. Share it on WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram.
> - RSVP or request-to-join. You approve guests, so you decide who comes.
> - Your address stays hidden. People see the area; the exact spot goes only to
>   approved guests.
> - A QR pass for each guest, saved on their phone, working without data.
> - Door check-in by scanning. Scanned the wrong person? Undo.
> - Your event is listed by city and category, so people outside your group can
>   find it.
>
> **How it works**
> 1. Send us your flyer, date, time and area. We create the listing.
> 2. Share your link. Guests RSVP and you approve.
> 3. At the door, scan the QR passes. You see who came.
>
> **What it costs:** nothing.
>
> **What it doesn't do:** PAMP does not take payments. If your event has an entry
> fee, you collect at the door as you do now.
>
> **Honest note:** PAMP is new. You'd be one of the first hosts, so we'll set it
> up with you personally.
>
> To start: WhatsApp [number] with your flyer and the date.

## 4. Organic social, pre-supply phase (goal: attract hosts, not attendees)

Every post ends with a host CTA such as "Hosting something in Lusaka? Message us
your flyer." Nothing mentions buying passes or paying.

1. **"The RSVP that lies."** Static card or 15s text-on-screen video. Hook: "80
   said they're coming. 25 came." Resolution: an RSVP you approve, with a QR you
   scan. Mark figures as illustrative.
2. **"Scan it at the door."** 20s screen recording of real QR check-in plus undo.
   The most credible content you have. Best for TikTok and Reels.
3. **"Your address isn't for everyone."** Carousel or video of the approximate pin
   and "request exact location". Speaks straight to house-party hosts.
4. **"Hosts wanted in Lusaka."** Plain text card on brand colours: what you're
   looking for (free events in the next 3 weeks), what you do (list it for them),
   what it costs (nothing). Pin it on Facebook.
5. **"We tested it at our own party."** Build-in-public: the first event, a door
   check-in, what broke and what was fixed. Whether to publish the real numbers
   (6 RSVPs, 1 check-in) is a user call `[A11]`.
6. **"No data? Your pass is already on your phone."** Short video toggling
   airplane mode and opening the QR pass. Real feature, real Zambian context.
7. **"Host spotlight" template.** Only after a real host agrees in writing. Hold
   until there is one. Do not fake it.
8. **"What's your worst door story?"** Question post. Builds engagement *and*
   produces a list of warm leads. Reply to every comment.

**Buffer free plan:** 3 channels × 10 scheduled = 30 slots. 8 concepts × 3
channels = 24 max, so it fits. Recommended: schedule only 4–5 per channel, leave
slots for reactive posts. Concept 7 held back. Concepts 2 and 6 are video —
confirm Buffer free supports TikTok video scheduling `[A12]`.

**Manual and free, outside Buffer:** post concept 4 in Lusaka Facebook groups
(check each group's rules); use concepts 1 and 4 as WhatsApp Status (manual).
Use the existing OG card and branded event image until Canva is signed in.

**Cadence:** Facebook 3/week, Instagram 2–3/week, TikTok 2/week.

## 5. The trigger — when to switch to consumer demand generation

Switch only when **all** are true on the same day:

**Supply**
1. ≥8 published, upcoming Lusaka events dated within the next 21 days.
2. ≥5 different hosts, with no single host holding more than 3 listings.
3. ≥3 categories represented.
4. Every listing has real artwork (not the default image) and a real description.
5. With the Lusaka filter on, Explore shows ≥4 events for any given week in the
   next 2 weeks.

**Proof it works**
6. ≥2 events have RSVPs from people outside the team and outside the host's own
   circle (found through Explore or search).
7. ≥1 non-team host has run a door check-in with ≥15 scans unaided.

**Product integrity**
8. All copy and in-app messaging is accurate about payments; no pass-buying
   prompts visible. `[A13]`
9. Kitwe and Ndola stay unpromoted until each has ≥3 events. The consumer
   campaign names Lusaka only.

**If the trigger isn't met by day 21:** do not start consumer posting. Diagnose
which archetype responded best and concentrate there; re-check weekly. If after 5
weeks there are fewer than 5 events, reconsider the host proposition — the
product, not the reach, is the problem.

**Rolling check after the switch:** if upcoming Lusaka events fall below 4 in the
next 14 days, pause consumer posts and return to host acquisition.

## 6. Brand doc input (pending sections)

### Audience

**Primary (now):** event hosts in Lusaka who organise events with their own guest
lists — house-party and private-venue hosts, student societies, church youth
teams, small promoters and DJ collectives, comedy and music-night organisers, run
clubs and pop-ups.
- *Want:* to know who's coming, to look organised, to avoid stress at the door.
- *Fear:* no-shows, gatecrashers, a public address, tech that fails when the
  network drops.
- *Language:* WhatsApp-first, short, practical. "How many are coming", "who's on
  the list". `[A14 — validate in the first 5 host conversations]`

**Secondary (after the trigger):** 18–35s in Lusaka, then Kitwe and Ndola,
looking for something to do this week and people to go with. Facebook and
WhatsApp first, then TikTok and Instagram. Data-conscious, phone-first.

**Not yet the audience:** anyone expecting to buy a ticket in the app.

### Voice and tone

A friendly organiser who knows the scene. Practical, warm, direct, a bit playful.
Never corporate.

1. **Plain words.** "Guest list", "RSVP", "door", "scan". Never "ecosystem",
   "solution", "platform", "seamless".
2. **Specific beats big.** Name the feature or the situation, not an adjective.
3. **Short sentences**, written to be read on a phone in a WhatsApp chat.
4. **Honest about being new.** Say "we're early" when it's true. No invented user
   numbers or testimonials.
5. **Zambian context, lightly.** Real places and situations (Lusaka, load
   shedding, data bundles). Local slang only when the writer is comfortable,
   never forced. `[A10]`
6. **Friendly without hype.** At most one exclamation mark per post, no all-caps.

*Never:* "game-changing", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "world-class",
"Zambia's #1", or any language about buying passes or paying through the app.

*Tone shifts:* hosts get calm and practical; attendees (later) get more playful.

### Taglines

- **Master:** "Find the party. Meet your people." — keep.
- **Host line:** "Your place. Your list."
- **Host alternate:** "Know who's coming."
- **Descriptor** (replaces the networking-hub line): "Events in Zambia, with a
  proper guest list." / "Free events, real guest lists." `[A4]`
- **Feature one-liners:** "RSVP. Approve. Scan." / "Your address stays yours
  until you say so." / "Your pass works without data."
- **Retire:** "Zambia's events & networking hub."

### Channels (supply phase priority)

1. **WhatsApp — manual only.** Host outreach, Status, the one-pager. The main
   conversion channel. No free API, so all manual and personal.
2. **Facebook Page** "Party at my place-PAMP" via Buffer. Recruitment posts,
   community groups (manual, per group rules), pinned "hosts wanted" post.
3. **Instagram** @pamp.partyatmyplace via Buffer. Visual demos and carousels;
   source of tagged-venue host discovery.
4. **TikTok** @party.at.my.place5 via Buffer if video scheduling is supported
   `[A12]`. Short demos. Lowest priority for hosts, highest for the later
   attendee phase.

*Not used:* paid ads, paid tools, WhatsApp broadcast tooling, any other platform.
*Rule:* nothing publishes without explicit approval.

### Content pillars

1. **Know who's coming** (host benefit): RSVP, approval, headcount — concepts 1, 4, 8.
2. **Your place, your rules** (privacy and control): hidden address, approval — concept 3.
3. **Door to done** (product in action): QR passes, scanning, undo, offline — concepts 2, 6.
4. **Built in Lusaka** (build-in-public): honest progress, fixes, first hosts — concepts 5, 7.

Later, after the trigger, add **"What's on"** (event highlights, city guides) for
attendees.

Supply-phase mix: 40% pillar 1, 20% pillar 2, 25% pillar 3, 15% pillar 4.

## Assumptions flagged

- **A1** — The user can do outreach and concierge listing personally, ~1–2 hrs/day for 3 weeks.
- **A2** — The user has a WhatsApp number for the one-pager and a plausible Lusaka network.
- **A3** — Zambians find events mainly via WhatsApp and Facebook and respond better to "events" than "networking". General market knowledge, not validated by data.
- **A4** — The new descriptor line and tagline alternates are untested. The user picks.
- **A5** — Unknown whether the app lets a team member create an event on a host's behalf or transfer ownership.
- **A6** — Whether to list pay-at-the-door events. Recommend free-entry first.
- **A7** — Suburb, campus and community names are unverified Lusaka references.
- **A8** — Funnel conversion rates are estimates, not benchmarks.
- **A9** — No competitor research was done; no competitor claims are made beyond the one noted. Verify before any comparison.
- **A10** — Scripts use casual English. Adding Bemba or Nyanja is a user choice, by a fluent speaker only.
- **A11** — Whether to publish the real first-event numbers (6 RSVPs, 1 check-in).
- **A12** — Buffer free may not support TikTok video scheduling. Check before relying on it.
- **A13** — Not verified that the app hides MoMo/Airtel options. Treat any visible payment UI as a risk to host trust.
- **A14** — Audience language and fears are inferred; validate in the first host conversations.
